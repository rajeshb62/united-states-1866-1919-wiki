United States: A History

Author: John Clark Ridpath

Completeness Assessment

The OCR ZIP appears complete as a single-volume illustrated popular history. It includes title pages, prefaces, full contents, major parts, Chapters I–LXXIV, late chapters through Cleveland’s second administration, conclusion, appendices/statistical material, and index-like end matter. The book is not focused on 1866–1919: only its final portion covers the post-Civil-War and late-nineteenth-century period.

Overview

Ridpath’s book is a popular, illustrated, one-volume national history written for a broad American readership. Its scope runs from pre-Columbian and European discovery through colonies, Revolution, early republic, Civil War, Reconstruction, Grant, Hayes, Garfield/Arthur, Cleveland, Harrison, and Cleveland’s second administration. It is especially useful for this wiki as evidence of how late-nineteenth-century popular history narrated the United States up to the 1890s.

For the 1866–1919 wiki, the main relevant material is in Chapters LXVII–LXXIV. Earlier chapters are useful only as background for long-run themes such as Union, expansion, sectional conflict, war memory, presidential chronology, and popular national narrative. The source should be paired with Paxson for later synthesis and with Blaine Volume II for congressional Reconstruction.

Narrative Source Flow

Prefaces and Contents

Lines 94–1476. Ridpath addresses the book to ordinary American readers and explains the goal of compressing the whole national story into an accessible single volume. The contents map the book into large parts: discovery, colonization, Revolution, national period, Civil War, and recent annals.

Chapters I–XXXVI — Discovery, Colonization, and Colonial Wars

Lines 3125–14305. These chapters cover Norse voyages, Spanish, French, English, and Dutch discovery, Virginia, Massachusetts, New York, Connecticut, Rhode Island, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Maryland, the Carolinas, Georgia, French and Indian War causes and campaigns, and the condition of the colonies before Revolution.

Chapters XXXVII–XLV — Revolution and Confederation

Lines 14552–18456. Ridpath narrates the causes of the Revolution, the beginning of armed conflict, 1776, campaigns of 1777–1781, France’s intervention, reverses and treason, Yorktown, and the weakness of the Confederation government before the Constitution.

Chapters XLVI–LX — Early Republic through Buchanan

Lines 18457–24271. This section proceeds administration by administration: Washington, Adams, Jefferson, Madison and the War of 1812, Monroe, John Quincy Adams, Jackson, Van Buren, Harrison/Tyler, Polk and the Mexican War, Taylor/Fillmore, Pierce, and Buchanan. It provides popular summaries of party development, expansion, Indian policy, banking, tariffs, Texas, Mexico, slavery controversy, and the approach of disunion.

Chapters LXI–LXVI — Lincoln and the Civil War

Lines 24272–27383. Ridpath covers Lincoln, causes of the Civil War, the first year of war, campaigns of 1862 and 1863, and the closing conflicts of 1864–1865. This cluster is useful background for Reconstruction and Civil War memory rather than a detailed military source.

Chapter LXVII — Johnson’s Administration, 1865–1869

Lines 27384–27778. This is the first directly postwar chapter. It covers Johnson’s accession, Reconstruction questions, conflict with Congress, the postwar settlement, and the beginning of the political order that shaped the wiki’s start date.

Chapter LXVIII — Grant’s Administration, 1869–1877

Lines 27779–31598. Ridpath’s Grant chapter covers the long Reconstruction decade: Grant’s presidency, Southern policy, political scandals, financial issues, party conflict, and the movement toward the contested election of 1876.

Chapter LXIX — Hayes’s Administration, 1877–1881

Lines 31599–32380. This chapter covers the Hayes presidency, the aftermath of the disputed election, Southern home rule, civil-service questions, monetary issues, railroad and labor unrest, and the transition away from high Reconstruction politics.

Chapter LXX — Administrations of Garfield and Arthur

Lines 32381–33601. Ridpath treats Garfield’s election, assassination, Arthur’s succession, patronage and civil-service reform, party conflict, and the administrative politics of the early 1880s.

Chapter LXXI — Cleveland’s Administration, 1885–1889

Lines 33602–34780. This chapter covers Cleveland’s first term: Democratic return to national power, reform language, tariff questions, pensions, public lands, and late-Gilded-Age party politics.

Chapter LXXII — Harrison’s Administration, 1889–1893

Lines 34781–37424. Ridpath covers Benjamin Harrison, the Fifty-First Congress, tariff and silver legislation, pensions, Republican policy, and the politics of the early 1890s.

Chapter LXXIII — Second Administration of Cleveland

Lines 37425–39454. The final substantive administration chapter treats Cleveland’s return, the Panic of 1893, monetary and tariff issues, labor unrest, and the crisis-ridden politics of the mid-1890s.

Chapter LXXIV — Conclusion

Lines 39455 onward. Ridpath closes with a reflective conclusion on the future of the American republic. It is useful as late-nineteenth-century popular civic rhetoric rather than as a factual endpoint for this wiki.

Coverage Guide

For this wiki, Ridpath should be mined selectively. Chapters LXVII–LXXIII provide compact popular coverage of Reconstruction through the 1890s. The earlier Civil War chapters can support Civil War-memory background, while the colonial and early republic sections are outside the period and should usually be ignored unless a page needs long-run context.

Notes on Scope
