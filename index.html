United States 1866–1919 Wiki

A narrative-research knowledge base on American history from Reconstruction after the Civil War through the end of the First World War era — constitutional reconstruction, federal and state politics, industrial capitalism, immigration and urbanization, westward expansion, labor and agrarian movements, Progressive reform, overseas empire, and wartime mobilization through 1919.

Total pages: 70

What follows is the map of the wiki — people, events, and concepts — then the source library on which every claim rests. Entity, event, and concept pages are built from those sources under a contemporary lens, without modern editorial framing.

Entities

Reconstruction executives

  • andrew-johnson — Seventeenth President: succession after Lincoln, presidential Reconstruction, breach with Congress, impeachment
  • grant-administration-and-reconstruction — Soldier-President: Stanton crisis path, Fifteenth Amendment, Southern restoration, two terms to 1876
  • hayes-administration — Seated after disputed 1876; reconciliation policy and close of federal Reconstruction phase

Gilded Age, imperial, and progressive executives

  • william-mckinley — Twenty-fifth President: gold standard victory of 1896, Spanish-American War, overseas empire, Buffalo reciprocity address, assassination
  • theodore-roosevelt — Square Deal, trust regulation, New Imperialism hinge, Bull Moose revolt
  • woodrow-wilson — New Freedom, neutrality and war, Fourteen Points, League fight

Industrial and financial titans

  • j-p-morgan-sr — Wall Street banker, syndicate organizer, railroad consolidator, 1895 gold rescue, U.S. Steel, Panic of 1907
  • john-d-rockefeller-sr — Founder of Standard Oil, pioneer of the industrial trust, horizontal and vertical integration, mass scientific philanthropy
  • andrew-carnegie — Scottish immigrant, master of Carnegie Steel, cost-accounting, Bessemer mass production, Gospel of Wealth
  • charles-a-lindbergh-sr — Insurgent Minnesota congressman, agrarian prosecutor of the Money Trust, Pujo resolution, anti-war critic

Corporate and banking institutions

  • house-of-morgan — Merchant banking partnership at 23 Wall Street; railroad reorganization, industrial consolidation, 1907 rescue, Allied purchasing
  • standard-oil-company — Premier industrial combination; Cleveland refining, pipelines, 26 Broadway, rebates, 1911 Supreme Court dissolution
  • carnegie-steel-company — Pittsburgh manufacturing partnership; Edgar Thomson works, Frick, vertical integration, Homestead, core of U.S. Steel

Events

Reconstruction settlement

  • reconstruction-acts — 1867 military districts, conventions, suffrage as condition of readmission
  • impeachment-of-andrew-johnson — Tenure crisis, House articles, Senate acquittal 1868
  • disputed-election-of-1876 — Contested returns; Hayes seated; bargain ending enforcement regime

Realignment, empire, and diplomacy

  • election-of-1896 — Sound money against free silver; realignment establishing Republican ascendancy
  • spanish-american-war — Swift defeat of Spain in Cuba, Puerto Rico, and Philippines; emergence of overseas power
  • treaty-of-paris-1898 — Cession of Puerto Rico, Guam, and Philippines for $20,000,000; Senate ratification battle
  • election-of-1900 — McKinley and Roosevelt defeat Bryan on the “full dinner pail” and imperialism
  • assassination-of-william-mckinley — Buffalo Pan-American shooting by Czolgosz; death and succession of Theodore Roosevelt
  • panama-canal-and-isthmian-diplomacy — Hay-Pauncefote to Goethals; isthmian hinge of New Imperialism
  • election-of-1912 — Taft–Roosevelt–Wilson; Progressive schism elects a Democrat
  • league-of-nations-debate — Covenant, Lodge reservations, Senate rejection, Wilson’s collapse

Panics, corporate mergers, and labor conflict

  • panic-of-1893 — Railroad insolvencies, European gold flight, unemployment, Coxey’s army, Pullman strike, silver repeal
  • gold-standard-crisis-of-1895 — Depletion of Treasury gold reserve, Morgan-Rothschild bond syndicate contract, populist backlash
  • homestead-strike — 1892 armed labor battle at Carnegie mill, Pinkertons, state militia, destruction of steel unionism
  • formation-of-united-states-steel — 1901 Morgan buyout of Carnegie Steel; first billion-dollar corporation consolidating steel trade
  • panic-of-1907 — Knickerbocker Trust collapse, Morgan’s library rescue pools, Tennessee Coal & Iron buyout, path to Federal Reserve
  • ludlow-massacre — 1914 Colorado coal strike fire, Rockefeller family crisis, Ivy Lee PR, and pioneering corporate welfare capitalism

Concepts

Constitution and party

  • thirteenth-amendment — Abolition as war result; federative system claimed still intact
  • fourteenth-amendment — Citizenship and representation; first condition of readmission
  • fifteenth-amendment — National bar on racial denial of the ballot
  • tenure-of-office-act — Senate curb on removals; hinge of the impeachment
  • congressional-reconstruction — Congress fixes terms of Southern return
  • republican-party-after-the-civil-war — Union-victory party become Reconstruction majority
  • popular-memory-of-reconstruction — Late-century schoolbook plot of Johnson–Grant–Hayes

Empire, commerce, and anti-imperialism

  • new-imperialism — Overseas power after Spain; Open Door and Caribbean
  • anti-imperialist-movement — League resistance against Philippine annexation, standing armies, and colonial despotism
  • philippine-policy — Military pacification of Aguinaldo, Schurman and Taft commissions, insular civil government
  • open-door-policy — Hay circulars, Boxer Rebellion relief, and defense of Chinese territorial integrity
  • teller-and-platt-amendments — Non-annexation pledge of 1898 and terms of Cuban protectorate of 1901
  • reciprocity-and-international-trade — Shift from insular protection toward reciprocal trade treaties to absorb industrial surplus

Finance, monopoly, and philanthropy

  • standard-oil-trust — Dodd’s 1882 legal device unifying multistate properties under nine trustees; catalyst for Sherman Antitrust Act
  • money-trust — Progressive critique of concentrated banking power; Pujo Committee exposure of $22 billion in interlocking directorates
  • gospel-of-wealth — Carnegie’s doctrine that surplus wealth is a sacred trust to be administered during life; library and educational benefactions
  • rockefeller-philanthropy — Gates’s scientific wholesale giving; University of Chicago, medical research, General Education Board, Rockefeller Foundation
  • banking-reform-and-the-federal-reserve-act — 1913 Glass-Owen legislation ending currency inelasticity and reserve-pyramiding via twelve regional banks

Progressivism and war

  • progressive-era — Regulation, exposure, insurgency, New Freedom
  • progressive-party — Bull Moose of 1912; Roosevelt–Johnson ticket
  • world-war-i-mobilization — Selective Service, boards, finance, home front 1917–18
  • fourteen-points — Wilson’s war aims and League as coping-stone
